Omega Glasses

An AI-powered assistive vision system designed to improve environmental awareness and independent navigation for visually impaired individuals.

Python Computer Vision YOLO Assistive Technology
Context
University of Ottawa | Engineering Design Project
Tools
Python, OpenCV, YOLO, Visual Studio, Speech Recognition
Timeline
September 2024 – December 2024
Recognition
Honorary Mention

The Problem

Individuals with visual impairments can face challenges identifying obstacles and navigating unfamiliar environments. The objective was to develop an accessible assistive system capable of detecting surrounding objects and communicating useful information through real-time audio feedback. The solution also needed to support intuitive, hands-free interaction and demonstrate the potential for integration with wearable hardware.

What I Did

  • Led a four-member team through the design, development, and testing of a computer vision-based assistive system.
  • Developed and tested a real-time object detection pipeline using Python, OpenCV, and YOLO, achieving approximately 90% detection accuracy during testing.
  • Implemented obstacle detection and audio notifications to alert users to objects in their surroundings.
  • Developed voice-controlled functionality to enable hands-free interaction with the system.
  • Contributed to the integration of computer vision software with the proposed wearable hardware platform.
  • Collaborated with team members to troubleshoot technical challenges, evaluate system performance, and refine the prototype.

Results

  • Developed a functional software prototype capable of identifying surrounding objects and providing real-time audio-based obstacle notifications.
  • Achieved approximately 90% object detection accuracy during testing.
  • Received an Honorary Mention for the project, recognizing its innovation and potential impact on accessibility.
  • Gained practical experience in computer vision, AI-assisted systems, team leadership, and interdisciplinary engineering design.
