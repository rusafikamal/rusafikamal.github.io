3D Topology Optimization Manufacturing: Automated CF Layup System

A mechanical design project focused on developing a passive feedback and disengagement mechanism for an automated carbon-fibre layup system. The design uses mechanical components to monitor material spooling and automatically interrupt power transmission once a specified threshold is reached.

CADMechanical DesignMechanical Analysis
Context
Course Project
Tools
SolidWorks, Mechanical Design, Mechanical Analysis

The problem

Automated carbon-fibre layup requires controlled material handling to maintain consistent operation. The objective was to develop a mechanical system capable of monitoring the spooling process and automatically disengaging the drivetrain when the spool reached a specified diameter of 25 mm, without relying on electronic sensors or active control systems

What I did

  • Designed a passive mechanical feedback mechanism in SolidWorks using a spring-loaded vertical follower to monitor changes in spool diameter.
  • Incorporated a snap-action trip arm to activate the disengagement mechanism when the spool reached the 25 mm diameter threshold.
  • Developed an interlocking dog clutch system to provide positive power transmission during normal operation.
  • Integrated a dual-bearing shifter fork mechanism to disengage the drivetrain automatically when triggered.
  • Refined component geometry and mechanical interactions to achieve a calculated system efficiency exceeding 85%.

Results

  • Developed a mechanical design integrating spool monitoring, threshold detection, and automated drivetrain disengagement into a single passive control system. The final design achieved a calculated mechanical efficiency greater than 85%, demonstrating the potential for reliable mechanical automation without electronic feedback. The project strengthened my experience in SolidWorks assembly design, mechanical power transmission, and the development of interconnected mechanisms.
